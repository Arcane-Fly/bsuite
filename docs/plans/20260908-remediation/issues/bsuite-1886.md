# [RULED] The GTO signs the training contract as employer — manually, off-system

https://github.com/GaryOcean428/bsuite/issues/1886

Snapshot updatedAt: 2026-08-24T03:26:11Z. Open at capture; re-read live.

## Operator ruling, 2026-08-10 — the training contract is signed manually

> "The GTO will sign it as the employer but **the training contract is generated by the STA so we have to sign it manually.**"

This completes the provenance model in bsuite#1885 and adds a constraint the classification alone did not carry.

### What it settles

The GTO **is** a signatory to the training contract — as the **employer**. That was the open question in §5 of the design doc, and the answer is yes.

But signing happens **off-system, on the STA's instrument**. So:

| | |
|---|---|
| `origin` | `uploaded` |
| `requires_signature` | **`false`** — meaning *false in CRM7*, not unsigned |
| What CRM7 does | records the uploaded, wet-signed document and verifies it |
| What CRM7 must never do | offer to generate it, or offer to e-sign it |

**The nuance worth keeping:** `requires_signature = false` here does not mean nobody signs it. It means **the platform is not the signing venue**. A training contract arriving in CRM7 should already bear the GTO's signature. If a future surface reads that flag as "no signature needed" and stops asking whether the uploaded copy is executed, that is a compliance gap — the flag governs *e-signing in CRM7*, nothing more.

A GTO whose system appears to generate or e-sign its own training contracts has a regulatory problem, not a UX one. That is why this is encoded in the data rather than left to the UI to remember.

### Still open from §5

- **Who signs an employment contract on the GTO side** — a role in the platform, or a named person?
- **Retention.** Uploaded regulator instruments and executed agreements almost certainly carry different statutory periods, and nothing in the platform models retention at all.

Refs bsuite#1885, bsuite#1871
