Implements **operator RULING 4.1 + 4.2 (2026-08-08)**. No tracked owner until now.

> **4.1** *"The provider table is deleted and replaced with a role entity. `aass_providers` records who, and has no column for what they do."*
> **4.2** *"`state_ir_config` gets the same treatment. 'The state industrial relations authority' is the entity. DMIRS is the current occupant in WA."*

## RULING 1.4 block — naming what gets deleted

**`aass_providers`** — 6 rows (MEGT, BUSY At Work, …), `abn` and `primary_contact_id` NULL on all 6.
Columns: `name, abn, operates_in_states, federal_registered, website`.
**Built for:** recording *which organisations* provide apprenticeship support.
**The defect:** it records **WHO** and has **no column for WHAT THEY DO**. So when the scheme was renamed the table's own name went stale and nothing in it captured the durable thing.
*Introducing commit: to be traced and stated in the PR before the drop — this block is not complete until it is.*

**`state_ir_config`** — 8 rows, AU state/territory IR authority names + URLs. Same shape: occupants, no role.

## The durable entities

**4.1 —** *the intermediary that lodges the training contract and administers employer incentives.* That role persists across **AASN → AASS → ACAP** (Apprentice Connect Australia Provider) and across the funding changes that came with each rename.

**4.2 —** *the state industrial relations authority.* DMIRS is WA's current occupant. Departments get renamed and merged.

Organisations become **ordinary editable records against the role, with effective dates**. A future rename is then a **data edit, not a schema change**.

## Effective dates are the load-bearing part
Not decoration. AASN, AASS and ACAP must resolve to the **same entity across three periods** so historical records filed under an old name still resolve. Three unrelated strings is the failure mode.
